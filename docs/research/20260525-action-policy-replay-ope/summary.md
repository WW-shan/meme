## Fetched Evidence

- Hudson & Thames, "Does Meta Labeling Add to Signal Efficacy?": meta-labeling only helps when the primary model is already useful and the meta model uses contextual, relevant features. The article also describes segregated train, validation, and out-of-sample data, and lagging features to avoid look-ahead bias.
- mlfinpy data labeling docs: triple-barrier labeling is path-dependent with profit, stop, and vertical barriers; meta-labeling uses a primary side signal plus a secondary take/pass model.
- Matthew Landers OPE notes: OPE estimates an evaluation policy from data generated by another behavior policy; core assumptions include consistency, no unmeasured confounding, and positivity/support. Importance sampling has support and variance problems; direct methods can be biased; doubly robust methods combine both.
- Thomas and Brunskill, "Data-Efficient Off-Policy Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning": OPE is useful where trying bad policies is costly; WDR/MAGIC-style estimators trade bias and variance and rely on behavior-policy support and importance weights.

`06-fetch-off-policy-bandit-arxiv.md` was mostly metadata and is not used for claim-level conclusions in this round.

## Application To This Bot

The prior action-policy meta-label probe is useful but not sufficient for optimization closeout. A selected precision result such as `19/25` positives is not a live or replay-equivalent decision because it does not estimate executed reward, drawdown, walk-forward robustness, or stress behavior under the 10% risk policy.

The next experiment should therefore evaluate a target action policy in a replay-style reward space:

- Use only decision-time features for policy selection.
- Keep ex-post path fields only as labels/rewards after the decision is frozen.
- Split sources chronologically so training, validation, final, and fresh-live slices do not leak.
- Require support by source family and action class before treating a result as actionable.
- Score policies by simulated reward or PnL, not precision alone.
- Compare against the current best v95/v84 live-sized baseline and keep the result shadow-only unless strict replay, walk-forward, and stress gates pass.

Because the live behavior policy did not actually buy rejected candidates, full causal OPE with reliable importance weights is support-limited. For these rejected fast-profit/then-collapse candidates, the honest next step is a direct-method market replay estimator: freeze the action policy at decision time, then use observed post-signal lifecycle paths to estimate reward. That can falsify the direction offline, but it must remain shadow-only until replay-integrated support is strong enough.

## Direction Ranking

1. **Replay-equivalent action-policy reward probe**: extend the current meta-label direction into direct reward/PnL evaluation for `quick_take_profit` versus `skip` on accepted and rejected candidate paths. This directly addresses the current live trigger: rejected candidates that hit `+25%` quickly and then collapse.
2. **Another rejected-only precision meta-label grid**: lower value. It repeats the prior probe shape and still lacks executed reward.
3. **Static quick-take-profit overlay**: reject. Prior work already warns against static quick-TP overlays, and research requires decision-time policy plus replay reward rather than a hardcoded rule.
4. **Global threshold or volume relaxation**: reject. Prior scoreboard entries already show this increases collapse exposure.

Selected direction: replay-equivalent action-policy reward probe, shadow-only, with a falsification rule based on validation/final reward and support rather than precision alone.

## Falsification Rule

Reject this direction for live/runtime use if the replay-style reward probe cannot show positive incremental reward on validation and final slices, cannot maintain source/action-family support, or only works by selecting a tiny unsupported pocket. Keep it as named shadow-only evidence if it improves diagnostic understanding but does not pass strict replay gates.
